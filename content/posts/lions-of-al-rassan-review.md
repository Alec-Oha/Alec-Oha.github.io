The Lions of Al-Rassan might be the best standalone fantasy novel I’ve read in a good while. I’ve rarely read a book this “short” that felt so long, and I mean that in the best way. Fantasy loves its trilogies, chronicles, and sprawling sagas, and I’m used to having a few books to settle into a world. This one moves at a breakneck pace and expects you to keep up. It reminded me of science fiction in the way it drops you into an unfamiliar world and trusts you to figure out how it works. Props to Guy “The Guy” Gavriel Kay.

The story follows a doctor, a poet, and a soldier. Each comes from a different faith, and you get a sense of the history they carry in how they talk to each other and what they owe the people back home. I especially loved the echoes of medieval Iberia and the way the desert feels present even when the story is in a city. The poetry made that world feel older and bigger than the story I was reading.

There are battles, poetry, a lil bit of romance, and a healthy dose of violence. By the end I felt like I’d read a whole series. Good punchy book.
